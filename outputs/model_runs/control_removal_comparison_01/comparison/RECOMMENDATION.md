Information criteria use the **full multinomial count**, K = number of design columns × two nonreference equations, including both intercepts. AIC = −2 log L + 2K; BIC = −2 log L + K log N, where N is cases. Negative differences favor the alternative. Compare values only within a regime on identical observations.

AIC, the primary criterion, ranks removal of both first in **each** regime. BIC agrees on the complete ranking and supports greater parsimony more strongly. There is no disagreement about the preferred specification, but the strength differs: democracy loses 2.581 log-likelihood units, versus only 0.354 in autocracy, for the same four-parameter reduction. Democracy's AIC advantage over either single removal is only about 1.5–1.6, so the exact ranking remains uncertain. Its 2.84-point improvement over the baseline is modest; autocracy's 7.29-point improvement is clearer. The common direction, BIC support, stable estimation, and limited focal sensitivity together justify the common simpler specification; no significance or preferred hypothesis result drove selection.

These mode indicators adjust for case composition, so removal has a substantive cost: speaking publicly and nonverbal expression may affect which disputes reach courts and how they are resolved. Neither is automatically dispensable because of a large p-value. Here both contribute little additional likelihood conditional on retained adjustment, and their removal has small effects on focal estimates and probabilities. Keeping legal system, court level, the two remaining expression modes, and time adjustment preserves broad institutional and case-context adjustment. This is an exploratory parsimonious association model, not evidence that the removed modes cannot confound a causal estimate; use the baseline sensitivity fit when interpreting adjustment dependence.

AIC and BIC are comparison aids rather than calibrated evidence under clustered observational sampling. The case-based BIC penalty does not replace a cluster-aware effective sample calculation, and these criteria do not correct omitted-variable bias. No criteria were added across regimes; countries overlap between samples.

## Coding, predictions, and cautions

`v2jupurge_lag1` is **not reverse-coded**: higher values mean fewer arbitrary judicial purges. The [V-Dem v16 codebook, section 3.8.1.2](https://www.v-dem.net/documents/70/codebook_v16.pdf) orders responses from massive arbitrary purges (0) to no judicial removals (4), then converts the scale to an interval measurement-model estimate. The fitted values are those interval estimates, not ordinal 0–4 scores. All 825 fitted cases were checked against the local V-Dem source by country ID and decision year minus one, with identical unreversed values. Thus moving right on the plot means less purging; the requested title remains “Judicial Purges.”

Predictions use the selected model's saved numerical state without refitting and preserve the existing calculations. Each focal variable is set to the grid value for every case, with other covariates retained, then probabilities are averaged over **each regime's own sample and observed predictor range**. The intervals are **pointwise 95%** normal delta-method intervals calculated on the logit of the averaged probability and back-transformed. They do **not account for model selection**. Saved checks confirm manual/library probability agreement, sums, and gradients; formatting never changes prediction arrays.

These plots are **not a formal test of differences between regimes**. Different support and covariate distributions make direct curve comparisons descriptive. Neither different subset significance nor the visually different slopes establish an interaction effect.

The fixed complete-case restriction preserves comparison but limits generalization; removing controls does not recover excluded cases. Of 1,075 source cases, 825 appear in these regime fits. Autocracy has only 17 mixed outcomes and sparse categorical outcome cells. Both samples have few country clusters (29 and 22); normal Wald uncertainty needs caution. The selected covariance ranks are 21 in each regime for 22 parameters, so full-vector joint Wald tests are unavailable, even though marginal SEs are finite and the covariance crosschecks pass. Country clustering does not remove observational confounding or uncertainty in source measurement.
